    Markov Attribution

    Fit a first-order transition matrix to ordered journeys. Allocate revenue in proportion to how much each channel’s removal changes the model’s conversion probability.

    Transitions and removal effects

    Count every transition from Start through the observed channels to Conversion or No Conversion. Divide by the total number of transitions out of that state. A first-order model uses only the current state to predict the next one. The two outcomes are absorbing and have a self-transition probability of 1.

    Solve for the probability of eventually reaching Conversion from Start. To remove a channel, redirect any transition into that channel to No Conversion and keep the other probabilities fixed. Divide the resulting drop by the baseline probability. Normalize the drops to allocate the entered revenue when their sum is positive.

    The scores describe dependence within the fitted journey model under this removal rule. Customers might switch channels if a campaign stopped. Estimate the effect of a real spending or delivery change using separate causal evidence.
