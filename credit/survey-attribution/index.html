    Survey Attribution

    A post-purchase survey records what responding buyers recall about discovery. Compare responses with platform credit after aligning the population, period, and channel definitions.

    What the survey measures

    A discovery question can surface mentions of word of mouth, podcasts, or offline media that have no trackable click. “Where did you first hear about us?” measures something different from “What prompted this purchase?”

    Memory, option order, and who responds all affect the results. A survey share describes reported discovery. Estimating how a channel changes purchases requires a credible comparison, such as a holdout.

    Enter the survey-attributed revenue among respondents, the response rate, and ad spend. The calculator divides survey revenue by the response fraction, then divides the resulting revenue by spend.     Survey quality checks

    Response rate
    Divide completed responses by eligible survey invitations. Compare participation across devices, order-value bands, or other groups where you know the eligible counts.
    Time to answer
    Review unusually fast or slow responses in context. Timing alone gives weak evidence about validity.
    Write-ins and multi-select
    Use write-ins to spot missing categories. When buyers can select several channels, report the share mentioning each channel and those shares can sum to more than 100%.

    Check wording and option order

    Randomly vary wording or option order among otherwise comparable buyers. Compare both completion and answer shares across versions, with uncertainty. Use the version that measures the question you intend to answer.

    Define the population eligible to respond and consider how their characteristics relate to who replies. For comparable groups and the role of overlap, see Overlap diagnostics.

    Desktop buyers make up 40.0% of eligible buyers and 52.6% of respondents. Most Facebook survey revenue comes from desktop respondents. Most Instagram and TikTok survey revenue comes from mobile respondents.

    Post-stratify the expansion

    Methodology divides all survey revenue by 38%, so every response stands for 1 ÷ 0.38 ≈ 2.63 eligible buyers. In this example, that gives desktop answers too much weight and mobile answers too little.

    Post-stratification divides each device group’s survey revenue by its own response rate. One desktop response stands for 4,000 ÷ 2,000 = 2.00 eligible buyers. One mobile response stands for 6,000 ÷ 1,800 ≈ 3.33.

    Post-stratified implied revenue = desktop survey revenue ÷ desktop response rate + mobile survey revenue ÷ mobile response rate

    For TikTok, $4,800 ÷ 50% + $15,000 ÷ 30% = $9,600 + $50,000 = $59,600.

    A group’s relative weight is its share of eligible buyers divided by its share of respondents. Using the displayed shares gives 40.0% ÷ 52.6% ≈ 0.76 for desktop and 60.0% ÷ 47.4% ≈ 1.27 for mobile. Dividing each unrounded relative weight by the 38% overall response rate gives the expansion weights of 2.00 and 3.33 buyers per response.

    With two device groups, weighting changes a channel’s implied revenue only if the groups differ in both response rate and average channel-attributed revenue per respondent. If both groups had responded at 38%, each response would stand for 2.63 buyers. If desktop and mobile respondents reported the same average revenue per respondent for a channel, the two implied revenue columns would also match. Facebook’s estimate falls because most of its survey revenue comes from the overrepresented desktop group.

    What the weights assume

    The weights use device alone. They assume that within each device group, respondents and nonrespondents have the same average revenue attributed to each channel.

    Mobile TikTok buyers are mobile buyers who first heard of the brand on TikTok. Of the 1,800 mobile respondents, 250 named TikTok and 1,550 named other channels. The control changes the TikTok group’s response rate relative to the other mobile buyers’ rate while holding the observed mobile response rate at 30%.

    When the control is k, other mobile buyers respond at r = (250 ÷ k + 1,550) ÷ 6,000, and mobile TikTok buyers respond at k × r. The scenario divides mobile TikTok revenue by k × r and mobile Facebook and Instagram revenue by r. It still divides desktop revenue by 50%. At 1.0×, both mobile rates are 30% and the scenario matches the post-stratified results.

    The scenario assumes that respondents and nonrespondents have the same average revenue within each group defined by device and discovery channel. If those averages differ, changing response probabilities alone cannot tell us how much revenue to attribute to each channel.

    TikTok’s implied ROAS ranges from 2.16x at 0.5× to 0.99x at 1.5×. The respondent counts, survey revenue and device response rates stay the same at every setting, so the survey data cannot distinguish these scenarios. Following up with randomly sampled mobile nonrespondents could help estimate differences in response probability and average revenue. Random sampling alone guarantees neither successful follow-up nor narrower uncertainty. Report the weighted estimate alongside the range of settings you consider plausible.

    Thin and empty groups

    Empty group. Suppose eligible buyers also included 1,000 app orders where the survey never appeared. With no app respondents, the group’s expansion weight is undefined. To estimate channel-attributed revenue for app buyers, survey them directly or state an assumption, such as that their average revenue by channel matches that of mobile web buyers.

    Thin group. Splitting mobile buyers into new and returning could leave 1,200 new mobile buyers with 40 responses. Each response would stand for 30 buyers, so a few answers could move the result. Compare weighted and unweighted estimates. Combining the groups makes individual responses less influential and can reduce variance. It can also introduce bias if the groups’ average channel-attributed revenue differs substantially.

    Models. With device as the only attribute, a model of TikTok revenue per respondent predicts $4,800 ÷ 2,000 = $2.40 for a desktop respondent and $15,000 ÷ 1,800 for a mobile respondent. Apply the group averages to all 10,000 eligible buyers and sum to get 4,000 × $2.40 + 6,000 × $15,000 ÷ 1,800 = $9,600 + $50,000 = $59,600, the post-stratified figure. A response-probability model using device alone estimates the same 50% and 30% response rates. Models can help when combinations of device, region and order value have few or no respondents. To make predictions for those combinations, the model needs assumptions about how the attributes combine. For example, it could assume that the difference between desktop and mobile is the same in every region.

    Survey Design Guide

    1. Define which purchases are eligible and whether you are measuring first discovery or influence on this purchase.
    2. Record eligible invitations and link responses to order IDs. Store question version, option order, response time, and the channel selections or write-ins.
    3. Plan collection around the precision and periods needed for your decision. Review participation and missing categories before aggregating.
    4. Align platform and survey periods and channel definitions before comparing figures. Use discrepancies to check tracking or prioritize a follow-up test.
