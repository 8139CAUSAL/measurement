    Contractual and Non-Contractual CLV

    Compare customer value under a contract, where churn is observed (a survival model), with repeat purchase without a contract, where churn has to be inferred (BG/NBD).

    Memoryless survival

    With exponential survival (a constant monthly hazard), a subscriber’s chance of staying for future months is independent of how long they have already stayed. For a customer who has stayed 12 months, value is computed from today, given that they are active now.

    Survival probabilities (exponential)

    Discounted expected value (accumulated)

    Formula: CLV = ∑ [ S(t + s | T > s) × $M × (1+d)−t ], summed over months 1 through 36 at a 1% monthly discount rate.

    This uses continuous exponential survival and a finite horizon. The subscription CLV formula uses a discrete monthly churn probability and an infinite horizon.

    BG/NBD Model — P(Alive) and E[Tx] use the closed-form BG/NBD equations from Fader, Hardie & Lee (2005). Hyperparameters (r, α, a, b) are assumed known; in production these would be estimated via maximum likelihood on your transaction log.

    Probability the customer is still active

    We don't know if they left. The BG/NBD model combines Recency, Frequency, and T with population-level hyperparameters to compute the posterior probability that a customer is still active.

    BG/NBD Formulas (Fader, Hardie & Lee 2005):
    P(Alive | x, tx, T) = 1 / (1 + δ) where δ = (a / (b + x − 1)) × (α + T)r+x / (α + tx)r+x
    E[Y(t) | x, tx, T] = ((a+b+x−1)/(a−1)) × [1 − ((α+T)/(α+T+t))r+x · 2F1(r+x, b+x; a+b+x−1; t/(α+T+t))] × P(Alive)   (Eq. 10, exact)
    Value = E[Y(t)] × AvgSpend
