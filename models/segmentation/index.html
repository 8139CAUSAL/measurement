    Segmentation

    Group customers by measured characteristics, behavior, or use context. Choose variables that are available when the decision is made.

    Define groups around a decision

    A retailer could group customers by purchase frequency and then compare seasonal patterns by region. An occasion-based grouping could distinguish weekday lunch purchases from weekend purchases. For example, define frequent buyers as customers with at least three orders in the prior 90 days. If 240 of 1,000 eligible customers qualify, the frequent-buyer segment is 24%. In a randomized offer test within this segment, 18 of 120 contacted and 12 of 120 controls purchasing yields a five-point lift. Compare that lift with the separately estimated effect in the other segment before claiming the offer works better for frequent buyers.

    Use PRIZM for an example of a geographic segmentation system.

    Segmentation Bases

    Demographics

    Age, income, education, occupation, household composition

    Geographics

    Region, urban/suburban/rural, climate, population density

    Psychographics

    Values, attitudes, lifestyle, personality, interests

    Behavioral

    Purchase frequency, brand loyalty, usage rate, benefits sought

    Technographic

    Technology adoption, device preferences, digital behavior

    Situational

    Usage context, time of day, occasion, accompanying others
