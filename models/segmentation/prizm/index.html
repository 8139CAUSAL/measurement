    PRIZM

    PRIZM groups households using demographic, geographic, and behavioral information. Check the level of the data you can join, such as household or postal code, before interpreting the assigned segment.

    Classification dimensions

    1 Socioeconomic Rank

    Segments are numbered by a composite socioeconomic rank based on the following factors.

    • Income level and sources
    • Educational attainment
    • Occupation type
    • Home value and ownership

    2 Social Groups

    Segments are grouped by urbanicity and socioeconomic status.

    • Urban
    • Suburban
    • Second City
    • Town & Rural

    These urbanicity categories are further differentiated by socioeconomic status.

    3 Lifestage Groups

    Further defined by age and household composition:

    • Younger vs. older households
    • Presence of children at home
    • Family size and structure

    Describe a market with segment shares

    A segment’s share is the fraction of an area’s households that belong to it. The household count is how many households that is. Report both when you compare a candidate site with an existing store’s trade area. A larger area can have the same share and many more households in the segment.

    A segment assigned by postal code describes the area as a whole, and the households in it vary. To estimate spending or profitability for individual customers, match your own customer records to segments at the household level. Before using a segment’s share to choose a site, check whether your existing stores with a higher share of that segment have higher sales.
