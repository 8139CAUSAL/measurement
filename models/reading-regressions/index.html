    Read a regression coefficient, its standard error, and the interval for the estimated relationship.

    Model Details
    Observations (n): 52 weekly data points  |  Predictors (k): 3  |  Degrees of Freedom (df): n − k − 1 = 48  |  Alpha (α): 0.05

    The Coefficient and Std Error columns come directly from the regression. Everything else—t-statistic, p-value, Significance, and the Confidence Interval bounds—is derived from those two numbers (plus the degrees of freedom). The remaining tabs show you exactly how.

    t-statistics & p-values

    The t-statistic

    The t-statistic measures how many standard errors the coefficient is away from zero. Larger absolute values mean the coefficient is more distinguishable from noise.

    Formula t = Coefficient / Standard Error

    For TV Spend: t = 3.20 / 0.80 = 4.00. For Social Spend: t = 1.40 / 1.50 = 0.93. The bigger the t-statistic, the stronger the evidence that the coefficient is not zero.

    From t-statistic to p-value

    The p-value answers: If the true coefficient were zero, how likely would we see a t-statistic this extreme (or more)? It uses the two-tailed t-distribution with df degrees of freedom.

    Two-tailed p-value p = 2 × P(T > |t|), where T ~ t-distribution(df)

    Significant at α = 0.05?

    Statistical significance

    Significance is a yes/no decision: compare the p-value to your chosen threshold (alpha, typically 0.05).

    Decision Rule If p-value ≤ α → Significant (reject H₀) If p-value > α → Not Significant (fail to reject H₀)

    Important
    “Not significant” does not mean the effect is zero. It means we don’t have enough evidence to confidently say it’s different from zero at this sample size and alpha level. A larger dataset or a different alpha could change the verdict.
    What This Means in Practice
    For TV Spend and Search Spend, the data provide strong evidence that the coefficients differ from zero. Sales varies with each channel after accounting for the others in the model. Interpreting a coefficient as the effect of spend requires a causal design. See Marketing Mix Modeling. For Social Spend, the coefficient is too imprecise relative to its size—we can’t distinguish it from noise at α = 0.05.

    Confidence intervals

    Margin of error

    The margin of error is the half-width of the interval. Multiply the coefficient’s standard error by the critical t-value for the chosen confidence level. Under the model assumptions, repeated intervals constructed this way cover the population coefficient at that rate.

    Margin of error MoE = t_critical(α, df) × Std Error

    In Excel: =TINV(0.05, 48) gives the two-tailed critical value. At df=48 and α=0.05, t_critical ≈ 2.011.

    Confidence interval

    Lower & Upper Bounds Lower 95% CI = Coefficient − MoE Upper 95% CI = Coefficient + MoE
    Percentage Margin of error %MoE = (MoE / |Coefficient|) × 100

    Reading the Plot
    The dot is the coefficient estimate. The horizontal bar is the 95% confidence interval. If the bar crosses the dashed zero line, we cannot rule out that the true coefficient is zero—the variable is not significant at α = 0.05. At all three levels, the TV and Search intervals exclude zero and the Social interval includes it, while each interval widens as the level rises.
