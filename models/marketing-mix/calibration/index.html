Calibrating an MMM with a lift test

How much should a randomized Search lift test change our spending plan?

Inputs
A fitted Search and Social model, a paired geo assignment ledger and an independent revenue-lift estimate.
Assumptions
Match the schedules, eligible markets, outcome window and units. Use independent evidence or model its dependence.
Prerequisites
Compare media schedules

Would you fund the Search increase?

In the second part of the experiment capstone, a retailer matches 16 eligible markets in eight pairs using their last 12 weeks of revenue. Within each pair a random draw assigns one market to increase Search from $2,000 to $3,000 per week for four weeks. Its partner stays at $2,000. Both spend $2,000 for two more weeks. The test measures that six-week window even if carryover persists. Social, unit costs and other planned inputs stay fixed. The 12 weeks before assignment run at the reference schedule, bringing effective exposure close to steady state.

The primary outcome is mean six-week incremental revenue per eligible market in US dollars. The pair is the independent comparison and pairs receive equal weight. The retailer sets a 60% contribution margin before media costs. It studies pre-test variation across pairs to plan precision. The account-level offer trial uses a different assignment unit and outcome.

Before the readout, name the assignment unit, eligible markets and weights, signed spend change, revenue window and Social policy. Recall the budget-lottery question. Assignment within pairs breaks the historical link between anticipated demand and which markets get the increase. With no interference across markets, the study estimates the effect of assignment. Interpreting that effect as the response to these spending paths also requires delivery as planned.

Assignment, delivered spend and revenue by pair

Reconcile invoices, credits, prepayments and delivery against the spend ledger. Follow all randomized markets as assigned. The eight paired differences produce one aggregate observation. Randomized paired-geo design and inference

Update the history-only fit

Use the lower-Search-prior fit from the schedule explorer. The lift likelihood compares the signed study estimate with each model’s signed difference for the same schedules, eligible markets and six-week horizon. The training and later-check outcomes precede the test. The test period supplies media histories for adstock and reserves its outcomes for the paired estimate.

study lift ~ Normal(model schedule difference, study SE²)

The second argument is variance. Use a finite, positive standard error. Halving it quadruples precision. The update also depends on the history and the other evidence. Sign matters in both revenue and spend. A Student-t likelihood can allow heavier tails.

Partial pooling transfers some information to the two observed markets outside the test through the shared Search mean. When is this update appropriate?

Record the assignment unit, eligible population and weights, both spend trajectories, other-channel policy, KPI, currency, measurement window, lift, SE and evidence source. A static curve contrast can use steady exposures when adstock is normalized. For ramps, cuts and washout, propagate each path from its preceding spend history and sum the weekly differences over the window. Convert the estimate, SE and model to the same units.

The paired cut uses independent outcomes in a later window, conditional on the same pairs. It has a new stabilization period at its $3,500 reference. The model carries each market’s earlier spend history into that period. The primary estimator averages post-assignment pair differences without a fitted pre-period adjustment.

A single matched lift constrains one contrast. Flexible curves and lag profiles can agree there and differ elsewhere. Compare intervals at the same credibility level and check sensitivity at the decision’s spend levels. Known truth gives absolute error for one run. Bias and coverage require repeated samples. MCMC diagnostics assess numerical estimation.

How an experiment becomes an ROI prior

An experiment-informed prior constrains a channel’s ROI before the media history enters the fit. The signed lift likelihood above instead compares each model draw with the study’s particular intervention. Meridian’s CalibrationBuilder is designed for average incremental ROI against a zero-spend baseline. The retailer’s $2,000-to-$3,000 contrast measures a finite return between two positive spend levels.

  1. Register an illustrative randomized Video holdout in pilot markets, with equal market weights and a zero-Video comparison, May 6–June 2, 2024. Across eligible pilot markets, the study assigns $40,000 over four weeks and estimates 1,000 incremental orders (SE 200). The reported rate is 0.025 orders per dollar (SE 0.005). At $60 incremental revenue per order, revenue ROI is 1.50 dollars per dollar (SE 0.30).
  2. Adjust for transport to the model window. Suppose the model’s average daily Video spend is twice the experiment’s and its last date is June 1, 2025, 52 weeks after the test. The spend and recency variance adjustments are each 1. With geometric decay of 0.5 and a maximum lag of eight weeks, four weeks capture about 94% of the modeled decay weight. Scaling by the inverse capture fraction moves 1.50 to 1.60. The spend, recency and missing-tail adjustments widen 0.30 to 0.53. The 52-week half-life applies to the recency factor. Weight in the combined distribution also depends on the study’s SE and the other evidence.
  3. Combine independent adjusted Normal readouts for the channel with a baseline prior. The builder uses a flat prior on positive ROI when the baseline is omitted. It approximates the combined density with a parametric family. The chart shows a Gamma approximation for Video and the default LogNormal for Social, with log-location 0.2 and log-SD 0.9. Social has no registered study or custom prior in this example.
Prior density values

How a cost-per-target benchmark adds different evidence

A separate study spends $48,000 and estimates 600 incremental orders. Its cost is $80 per incremental order, with a supplied SE of $10 per order. A modeled 500 incremental orders would imply $96 per order. A platform CPA of $48 from 1,000 attributed orders uses a different denominator. Revenue ROAS also requires a revenue value per incremental order.

Switch display units for the same likelihood. Find the row for $96 per order and compare its support at the corresponding orders per $1,000. Then change the measurement assumption. The plot shows experimental evidence alone, scaled to a maximum of 1 in each curve.

Use matched totals. With fixed incremental spend, divide revenue-lift SE by the absolute spend change. If both totals are estimated, account for their joint uncertainty at the assignment-unit level. At a fixed revenue value of k dollars per order, incremental CPA equals k divided by revenue iROAS. Away from zero, the first-order SE for 1/r is SE(r)/r² before scaling the units. A signed Normal lift permits negative readouts. A LogNormal readout requires a positive observation. A zero denominator makes a ratio undefined. Near zero, reciprocal intervals can be unbounded. Use signed lift in those cases, and request the study contrast if a headline ratio lacks its intervention or denominator. Paired-geo inference for incremental return

Reproduce the 104-week benchmark

Weekly spend is in thousands of dollars. Set g(v)=v/(v+15) and Search spend s=4+0.4v+e. At each Video spend level (5, 15, 25 and 45), choose thirteen weeks with e=+4 and thirteen with e=−4. Set outcomes to their additive model means 1,000+240g(v)+4s. Use flat coefficient priors and known outcome SD 100. These are fixed design values. A random sample would have different realized moments.

The direct contrast is δ=0.125θᵥ. Regressing transformed Video on Search and an intercept leaves enough variation for a history-only SD of 10.618 orders/week. The VIF is 2.56 from the correlation between g(v) and s. Media account for 15.2% of fitted orders. Precision depends on residualized variation and outcome noise.

An independent study with SE 3 adds inverse-variance precision, gets 92.61% weight and yields posterior SD 2.887. Applying the responsive readout to the direct contrast puts direct truth 4.65 posterior SDs below the mean, or 2.69 SDs below the lower 95% endpoint. The intercept offsets the changes so the 104-week sum stays at 122,668 fitted orders. Weekly fitted values, residuals and predictive scores can move.

The 0.4 spend response represents an assumed Search policy. In real studies, fixed Search spend can still allow changes in queries, organic traffic and click mix. Conditioning on Search alone cannot identify a direct effect. A total-effect analysis needs a defensible design and a response that permits both the saturating Video term and the linear induced Search term. Subtracting a mediated lift requires identification and joint uncertainty. Trace the media funnel and compare channel removals in the contribution waterfall.
