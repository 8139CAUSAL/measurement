    Calibrating an MMM with a Lift Test

    Paired Search geo test

    A retailer randomly assigns one market in each of eight matched pairs to raise weekly Search spend from $2,000 to $3,000 for four weeks. Both markets then spend $2,000 for two weeks. Compare six-week revenue per market, weight pairs equally, and keep Social spend fixed.

    Interpret the result as the effect of the planned spend change if delivery followed assignment and markets did not affect each other.

    Assignment, delivered spend and revenue by pair

    The update uses one aggregate lift estimate and its standard error from the eight paired differences.

    Check whether the test matches the model contrast

    Update the history-only fit

    Update the lower-Search-prior fit with the signed lift from the same markets, spend schedules, and six-week window. The fit uses earlier outcomes. The study-period outcomes enter through the lift estimate.

    study lift ~ Normal(model schedule difference, study SE²)

    The second argument is variance. A smaller standard error gives the test more weight. Model evidence jointly if it reuses outcomes or controls.

    Check the model against a later Search cut

    The reserved prediction uses the increase test at its reported precision and includes the cut study’s sampling error. Adding the cut to the fit makes the comparison in-sample.

    Search response before and after the update

    The gray and blue bands show 95% credible intervals before and after calibration. The update changes the curve’s scale while holding decay and shape fixed.

    Search response by spend level

    The untested-market curves borrow information through partial pooling. Applying them to another market requires evidence that the response transfers.

    One test constrains the response over its tested spend range. Tests at other spend levels can help distinguish curves that agree over that range.

    Reallocate a fixed $4,400 weekly budget

    Compare a new four-week split with $2,200 per channel. Both plans return to the original split for two more weeks. Rank plans by expected six-week revenue per market, holding total spend and contribution margin fixed.

    Revenue gain by budget split

    The test changes Search only. Changing both channels requires a model projection that depends on the fitted response, carryover, and channel interactions.

    Other calibration examples

    When a test measures the wrong effect

    In this synthetic meal-kit example, increasing Video adds 30 orders per week with Search fixed, or 46 when Search spend also rises. Using the joint estimate for the fixed-Search contrast shifts modeled credit from Search to Video.

    How an experiment becomes an ROI prior

    A prior uses experiment evidence before fitting the media history. Meridian’s calibration workflow is designed for average ROI against a zero-spend baseline. The Search example above uses a likelihood for a specific increase from positive spend.

    In this separate illustration, a $40,000 Video holdout estimates 1,000 incremental orders (SE 200). At $60 of revenue per order, the starting ROI is 1.50 (SE 0.30). The example shifts and widens it to about 1.60 for differences in timing, spend, and captured carryover. The chart shows the resulting Video Gamma approximation (mean 1.60, SD 0.58) and a default Social prior.

    Prior density values
    Compare cost per order and orders per dollar

    A separate study spends $48,000 and estimates 600 incremental orders. Its cost is $80 per incremental order with a reported SE of $10.

    Changing display units preserves the likelihood at corresponding values. Changing the assumed measurement error can change the likelihood. Each curve has a maximum of 1.

    Use signed lift when a ratio denominator is zero or too uncertain. With fixed incremental spend, divide the revenue-lift SE by the absolute spend change. If both quantities are estimated, account for their joint uncertainty.
