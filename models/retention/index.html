    Retention

    Compare retention at the same elapsed time for signup cohorts. Use a common definition of churn and a known end date for the data.

    Reading retention data

    1. Cohort analysis

    Group customers by signup month and track retention rates over time.
    Compare cohorts at the same age. Differences can reflect who signed up, seasonality, or changes to the product. Use a credible comparison to attribute a difference to a particular change.

    2. Engagement scoring

    Build composite scores from product usage, feature adoption, and interaction frequency.
    Use product activity recorded before the prediction date. Check whether the score predicts retention in later cohorts using a defined follow-up window.

    3. Milestone tracking

    Monitor key activation events that correlate with long-term retention.
    An early action can help predict retention. Test an onboarding change separately to learn whether encouraging that action improves retention.

    4. Attrition diagnostics

    Compare when churn happens and which reasons customers give, to find changes worth testing.
    Compare when churn occurs and which reasons customers report. Use the patterns to form hypotheses and evaluate proposed changes with a holdout or another credible comparison.

    Cohort retention curve

    Enter the remaining members of one original cohort each month. The tool summarizes month-to-month rates and plots the share of the original cohort still active.

    Month 0 = starting count. Each subsequent number = customers remaining at that month.

    Counts cannot increase. Average monthly rates give each month with customers at risk equal weight. Mean observed lifetime sums survival at the start of each month in the observed window.
