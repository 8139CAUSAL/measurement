Hold impressions at 1,000,000 and CPM at $10 ($10,000 total). Compare 500,000 people reached twice with 100,000 reached ten times. The source reports average frequencies from 1.5 to 10. The grid uses quarter-point steps and caps reach at the eligible market population.

Frequency, reach and modeled response table

The model assumes linear reach at fixed frequency and applies adstock to reach × Hill(frequency). The chart shows the per-person curve. At fixed impressions, dividing impressions among more people trades frequency for reach. On the feasible grid the best point can be at a boundary. Reach counts each person once within the stated period and channel. Average frequency hides the distribution of exposures and says nothing about cross-channel audience overlap. Apply saturation before or after adstock

Move the decay slider above to compare the same $2,000 pulse and $0 follow-up with a zero pre-period. These examples use normalized adstock and the same illustrative logistic response $4,000 × tanh(exposure / $2,000).

In the unnormalized recursion A[t] = x[t] + αA[t−1], identical previous histories give a first difference of exactly Δx. A sustained change builds toward Δx/(1−α) for 0 ≤ α < 1. Finite lags use the finite sum of weights. Multiplying the new input by (1−α) gives the normalized recursion, whose steady-state gain is one.
