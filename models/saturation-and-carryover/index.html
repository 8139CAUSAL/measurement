    Carryover Effect

    The carryover rate λ is the fraction of exposure retained each week. The half-life is the time it takes the remaining exposure from a single spend pulse to fall by half.

    Apply saturation before or after adstock

    Move the carryover slider above to compare the same $2,000 pulse and $0 follow-up with a zero pre-period. These examples use normalized adstock and the same illustrative tanh saturation $4,000 × tanh(exposure / $2,000).

    In the unnormalized recursion A[t] = x[t] + αA[t−1], identical previous histories give a first difference of exactly Δx. A sustained change builds toward Δx/(1−α) for 0 ≤ α < 1. Finite lags use the finite sum of weights. Multiplying the new input by (1−α) gives the normalized recursion, whose steady-state gain is one. Start each geography with its own history and apply the same scaling and lags to both schedules.
