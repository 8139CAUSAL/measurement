Causal Graphs and Identification

Which comparisons can identify an intervention’s effect from observational data?

Inputs
A unit, a well-defined intervention, a target population, a follow-up window, and a plausible causal graph.
Assumptions
Identification depends on the graph, consistency, and support for the comparisons the graph requires.
Prerequisites
Incrementality

Graph and adjustment

Define the unit, intervention, outcome, follow-up and target before choosing a comparison. In the synthetic example below, each unit can receive a one-time intervention at enrollment. The outcome is an event within 30 days. The target can be all eligible units or those who actually received treatment.

Each unit has potential outcomes Y(1) under treatment and Y(0) under control. The average treatment effect (ATE) averages their difference across the eligible population. The average treatment effect on the treated (ATT) averages it over the population that received treatment. Identification asks whether the assumptions express that target using observable quantities. Estimation uses data to approximate those quantities.

When does adjustment identify a total effect?

A directed causal path points forward along every edge. A path such as T → C ← U → Y starts outward and can still carry a noncausal association after conditioning on C. A collider blocks a path until you condition on it or on one of its descendants.

The back-door criterion is a sufficient adjustment rule. Choose a set with no descendants of treatment that blocks every path entering treatment through an incoming arrow. Use consistency between the specified intervention and observed outcomes, conditional exchangeability given the set, and treatment support for the target covariate values. Standardize the conditional arm means to the same target distribution.

Conditioning on a mediator can remove part of the total effect and can introduce bias through shared causes of the mediator and outcome. A controlled direct effect requires a specified intervention on the mediator and additional identification assumptions.

Specify the graph and estimand, identify the comparison, estimate it, then stress-test the assumptions. A placebo outcome or shuffled-label check can uncover a problem if the outcome or reference distribution respects the design. An unmeasured common cause can remain even after the check passes. Pearl’s causal inference overview

Use Propensity weighting or Matching when baseline adjustment is credible. Compare changes over time with Difference-in-Differences, or compare scores near an eligibility threshold with Regression Discontinuity. Use Causal comparison design chooser to check which design fits your data.

Identification through a mediator

Suppose hidden U affects treatment T and outcome Y, but its influence on the mediator M runs through treatment. At each measured baseline context X, M must intercept every directed T-to-Y path. X must block any back-door path from T to M, and T with X must block every back-door path from M to Y. Check support for each comparison.

Compute the front-door contrast and examine one-sided support

Within X, average each observed outcome mean at mediator value m over the observed treatment distribution. Then average these quantities using P(M = m | T = a, X) to get the interventional mean for arm a. Average those means across the eligible context distribution for the ATE.

With known M(0) = 0 for every unit and the stated causal assumptions, the ATT can use the observed outcome mean among all treated units minus the mean of treated units with M = 0 in each context, weighted by the treated context distribution. Any treated context with no M = 0 comparison prevents this calculation.

Opt-in can leave hidden traits varying within the observed uptake group. A product of slopes requires compatible linear additive models. Varying an ambiguously timed control is a sensitivity analysis without a guaranteed ordering or causal bounds. When estimating uncertainty, resample units or clusters at the level of independent variation. Pearl’s front-door identification formula
