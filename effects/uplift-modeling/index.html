    Uplift Modeling

    Estimate how much an intervention changes average conversion for customers with similar traits measured before treatment.

    1 Randomized experiment

    Randomly assign customers to the campaign or a defined control. Compare outcomes by assignment, using traits measured before the campaign to describe how the average effect varies.

    Key Definition

    Uplift is the difference between conversion probabilities under treatment and control for a baseline profile. Only one outcome is observed for each customer.

    2 Target transformation

    Two ways to estimate conversion lift by baseline profile are shown below.

    T-Learner: Train separate models for treatment and control, then subtract predictions.

    Class Transformation: Combine assignment and the observed outcome into a target whose conditional expectation can estimate uplift under the known randomization probabilities.

    3 Scoring

    Score a profile using its predicted conversion rates under the two assignments.

    Predicted uplift = predicted conversion under treatment − predicted conversion under control

    These four hypothetical response types describe what a customer would do under both assignments. A model can estimate the average lift for a profile, even when its individual response types are unknown.

    Sleeping Dogs
    Would convert under control and would not convert under treatment.
    Sure Things
    Would convert under either assignment.
    Lost Causes
    Would convert under neither assignment.
    Persuadables
    Would convert under treatment and would not convert under control.

    Reading the Qini curve. On evaluation data, the curve summarizes cumulative estimated lift as you contact more people in score order. The curves shown here are illustrative.
    • The diagonal is random targeting — no model, just chance.
    • A good ranking targets positive lift first. The example peaks at 70% and declines as it adds customers with negative predicted lift. Contact cost can move the best stopping point earlier.
    • A bad model dips below the diagonal, indicating less estimated lift than random targeting at that share.
    • The Qini coefficient is the signed area between the model and random targeting. The calculation uses the targeted fraction from 0 to 1 and divides cumulative conversions by the 100-conversion full-population lift. Area below the random line counts negatively. AUUC is the area under the model curve.

    Observational targeting

    A conditional average treatment effect (CATE) averages Y(1) − Y(0) at a specified baseline profile. With observational data, justify exchangeability and overlap for the treatment and outcome before training a targeting model. This synthetic example uses separate development and policy-evaluation records.

    Trace out-of-fold predictions

    The small development example predicts mean outcome E[Y | X] and treatment probability E[T | X] by profile using the other two folds. Each row is scored only when it is held out. The table shows the training rows, scored rows, and the frozen training-only means used to score the selected fold.

    Development folds and nuisance predictions

    Residual-on-residual fitting compares Y − m(X) with T − e(X). Cross-fitting estimates the outcome and treatment models on separate folds from the records they score. An orthogonal score makes the estimated effect locally insensitive to small errors in those models. This protection requires overlap, a suitable score and sufficiently accurate models. If effects also vary with auxiliary covariates, the residual slope within a displayed profile can weight effects by treatment variance. Specify the effect model or standardize contrasts to the target distribution.
