Analyzing A/B Tests

Which estimate and interval answer the decision the experiment was designed to inform?

Inputs
Randomized IDs, eligibility rules, outcome counts or unit-level values, and a fixed observation window.
Assumptions
Prespecify the estimand and uncertainty method. Align follow-up and use the randomized unit for inference.
Prerequisites
Randomized Assignment and Blocking, P-Values

Specify the unit, denominator and window

Enroll eligible accounts and randomize by account. If the endpoint is 30-day conversion, record one measurable yes/no outcome for every assigned account, starting the clock at assignment. Count every eligible assigned account in its assigned arm, including unopened offers. Record missing follow-up separately from observed nonconversion. If exposure can spill across accounts or assignment happens by household, account for the actual randomized unit when estimating uncertainty.

Document eligibility, the event and join ID, the window, and the analysis before seeing outcomes. Define the KPI in Plan Builder. For binary conversion, go straight to Estimate and interval. The continuous example below uses positive spend per account.

Inspect skew and the sampling distribution

Kernel density estimate

Normal Q–Q plot

The Gaussian kernel can put density below the zero-dollar boundary. The highlighted rightmost point shows the upper tail relative to the Q–Q reference line. A mean answers an average-value decision. A median describes a typical account, and a gap between them alone cannot identify outliers.

Check comparable groups or model residuals. Pooling can hide group differences or create a mixture that departs from normality. A pooled mixture can also be normal, as when its component distributions are identical. Look at sample size and tail shape alongside Shapiro–Wilk and Anderson–Darling, which emphasizes the tails. A large W, a nonsignificant test or a close visual fit cannot establish normality. The usual central limit argument for means requires independent units and finite variance. Extreme values and skew can slow convergence. NIST guidance on distribution checks.

For an observational comparison of activity and spending, see the Pearson example in Path Analysis correlation matrix.

Choose the method by the question

Enter data for independent arms with complete follow-up. The contrast is offer minus control in percentage points. Relative lift divides the contrast by the control rate and is undefined when that rate is zero.

Repeat samples from known rates

Keep the assigned counts above and specify the rates that generate each binomial sample. View 300 independently simulated 95% Newcombe intervals.

Wilson gives uncertainty for an individual arm rate. Newcombe’s hybrid-score method combines Wilson limits to estimate uncertainty for the difference. The Wald interval can collapse when observed rates are zero or one. Overlap of the arm intervals cannot decide a test of their difference. Coverage is how often a procedure includes the true difference in repeated samples under stated assumptions, and it can vary with the true rates. Independent-proportions interval methods.

The pooled z-test is a separate test of a zero difference. Its two-sided p-value equals the uncorrected Pearson chi-square p-value for the same 2×2 outcome table with one degree of freedom. A sample ratio mismatch (SRM) instead checks assignment counts against planned allocation. Sparse expected cells can make the z approximation unreliable.

A nonsignificant result can still include a meaningful lift

For confidence intervals and critical values, see Regression confidence intervals and the t-Distribution. For several arms or outcomes, use Multiple comparisons across arms.

Check whom and when the estimate represents

Both activity bands have a five-point offer advantage. Change the composition of the two arms and see whether pooling reverses the sign. These segments were defined before assignment. Randomization balances segment mix in expectation.

If one arm has no observed accounts in a band, the synthetic rates still show what standardization would do. Estimating that rate in a real experiment would require observations or a modeling assumption.

Read the same trial by day and cumulatively

The 21-day trial enrolls Monday through Sunday for three weeks. Each daily contrast uses the cohort assigned that day, with a common seven-day outcome window from first exposure opportunity. The last cohort matures seven days after the end of recruitment.

Daily cohorts, exposure timing and estimates

Novelty or learning could change the effect as exposure accumulates, and weekday demand and traffic can also move the estimate. Whole weeks include every weekday, while traffic determines their weights. Align calendar periods and exposure windows across arms. A changing daily contrast alone cannot distinguish these explanations from noise. See P-Hacking and Replication before choosing a cutoff after looking at a cumulative plot.
