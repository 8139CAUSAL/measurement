    Estimate Effects

    Estimate incremental effects using experiments or observational comparisons.

    Randomized experiments

    • IncrementalityCompare attributed revenue with lift from a randomized holdout.
    • Randomized Assignment and BlockingSimulate randomization, baseline balance, and blocking.
    • P-ValuesInterpret a p-value alongside effect size and uncertainty.
    • Sample Size & PowerPlan sample sizes for means, binary outcomes, and paired pre/post measurements.
    • P-Hacking and ReplicationSee how analysis choices, multiple testing, and replication affect reported results.
    • Analyzing A/B TestsEstimate conversion lift and uncertainty. Inspect group mix and continuous outcomes.
    • Offer ExperimentFollow a randomized offer test from assignment to a decision against break-even.
    • Incremental Lift ReportCalculate overall and segment-level lift from exposures, responses, and revenue.
    • Uplift ModelingEstimate how average treatment effects vary across customer profiles.

    Observational methods

    • Causal Graphs and IdentificationUse a graph to decide which variables to adjust for and when.
    • Propensity Scores, Matching, and WeightingEstimate treatment probabilities, check overlap, and build a comparison group by matching or weighting.
    • Difference-in-DifferencesCompare changes in treated and comparison groups over time.
    • Regression DiscontinuityEstimate a local effect around an eligibility cutoff.
    • Heckman Selection CorrectionAdjust a spending model when customer outcomes are selectively observed.

    Choose a modeling approach

    • Propensity scoresEstimate treatment probability from pre-treatment characteristics to check overlap, match, or weight groups. Causal interpretation requires that measured covariates account for confounding.
    • Churn predictionEstimate churn probability over a defined window. Test whether a retention offer changes outcomes separately.
    • Uplift ModelingEstimate average treatment effects for similar baseline profiles. Use randomized data or credible adjustment and overlap, then compare estimated benefit with contact cost.
