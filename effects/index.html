Choose a response method

Propensity Scores and Balance

Observational data is riddled with selection bias. Treated and untreated groups differ systematically, so naive comparisons confound the true effect with pre-existing differences.

Simulate a randomized experiment after the fact. Propensity scores estimate each unit's likelihood of treatment, then re-weight or match the data so treated and control groups are balanced on observed covariates.

Inputs Treatment assignment and pre-treatment characteristics.

Churn prediction

Estimate the probability of a customer leaving within a defined window.

Inputs Historical activity, known churn outcomes, and predictors available at scoring time.

Uplift Modeling

Standard response models predict who will convert, but they can't distinguish genuine incrementality from "Sure Things" who would have converted anyway.

Predict the incremental lift caused by treatment for each individual. Isolate the "Persuadables" — the segment where marketing spend actually moves the needle — and stop wasting budget on everyone else.

Inputs Randomized views use randomized treatment assignment, outcomes, and features available before contact. Observational targeting uses historical treatment, outcomes, and baseline covariates sufficient for conditional exchangeability and overlap.

See survey response weighting for a related use of response probabilities.
