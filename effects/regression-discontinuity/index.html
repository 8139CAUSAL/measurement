Regression Discontinuity

What changes at a documented eligibility cutoff?

Inputs
The running score, eligibility rule, outcomes on both sides, and receipt for a fuzzy design.
Assumptions
Untreated and treated potential-outcome means must be continuous at the cutoff for the respective local contrasts.
Prerequisites
Causal Graphs and Identification

Compare observations near an eligibility cutoff

A service awards a fee waiver when an application score reaches 50. The running variable is the score, and the first eligible score is 50. Compare the fitted mean outcome just above 50 with the fitted mean just below. Continuity means that, absent the change in eligibility, each relevant potential-outcome mean would change smoothly through 50.

With deterministic receipt, eligibility and receipt jump together. The outcome jump identifies a local effect at the cutoff. With incomplete take-up, the outcome jump describes the local effect of eligibility, including the actual uptake response. The jump in receipt probability is the first stage.

For a fuzzy RD receipt effect, divide the outcome jump by the receipt jump. Continuity supplies the local eligibility comparison. Exclusion requires eligibility to affect outcomes only through receipt, and monotonicity rules out applicants whom eligibility causes to stop receiving the waiver. Under those assumptions and a nonzero first stage, the ratio describes applicants at the cutoff whose receipt changes with eligibility. See Encouragement and uptake.

Cutoff explorer

Change the receipt scenario or the bandwidth. Bins show observed means. The triangular-weighted local linear fits use the underlying observations, with a separate slope and intercept on each side. Vertical dashed lines mark the eligibility cutoff.

At the default 10-point bandwidth, the deterministic scenario has an outcome jump of +7.29 units and a receipt jump of +100 percentage points. Eligibility and receipt coincide at score 50.

Inspect binned observations, support and fit values

Look for a change in score density and jumps in pre-treatment covariates. The explorer shows counts in equal-width bins and estimates a jump in a baseline covariate. In real data, check whether applicants or staff could sort precisely around 50, whether eligibility triggers another benefit at the same score, and whether the two sides have support. A density check or dropping observations around the cutoff cannot establish comparability.

Use bandwidth sensitivity to see how nearby information and precision change the answer. The plot shows the local linear estimates. The 95% intervals use a bias correction from a quadratic fit and a robust standard error. With sparse support, a first-stage interval can extend beyond feasible probability differences. A zero population receipt jump leaves the receipt-effect ratio undefined. A small or imprecise estimate makes conventional ratio inference unreliable. The eligibility outcome jump still answers a policy question under continuity. Calonico, Cattaneo & Titiunik · Feir, Lemieux & Marmer on weak fuzzy RD
