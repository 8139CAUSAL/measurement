Worked Example · Loyalty Program Lift

What lift estimate do we get after matching customers and subtracting the comparison trend?

Inputs
Pre-join customer characteristics and average group spend before and after enrollment.
Assumptions
Assess match quality and the parallel-trends assumption before interpreting lift.
Prerequisites
Matching, Difference-in-Differences

Match members to similar non-members on measured characteristics, then compare how their spending changes.

🔄

Pre-Join Matching

Members and non-members are matched based on their behavior before the member joined — transactional characteristics, frequency, and order value.

👥

Nearest Neighbor Matching

Match individual members to non-members with similar pre-join behavior. Compare changes in spending over the same period.

🎛️

Control Variables

Include relevant pre-join characteristics such as location or household behavior. Check overlap and balance after matching.

1 Propensity Score Matching

Calculate a single score representing each customer’s probability of joining, then match members to non-members with similar scores.

  • Reduces dimensionality — one score vs. many variables
  • Creates balanced comparison groups on observable characteristics
  • Assumes no unobserved confounders (unlike Heckman)
  • Works well when you have rich pre-join behavioral data

2 Difference-in-Differences

Compare the change in spending for members vs. matched non-members from pre- to post-period.

  • Controls for time-invariant differences between groups
  • Accounts for external trends affecting everyone equally
  • Estimated program lift = (Member post − Member pre) − (Control post − Control pre)
  • Requires comparable untreated spending changes, no anticipation, unexposed controls, and comparable group composition

Difference-in-Differences Lift Calculator

Enter average monthly spend for members and matched controls before and after the join date. At the defaults, members rise from $100 to $130 and controls from $98 to $108. The difference in changes gives an estimated program lift of $20 per member per month. An interval also requires information about sampling variation and dependence.

Review the assumptions for interpreting the estimate.
