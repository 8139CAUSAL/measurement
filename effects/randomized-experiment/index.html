    Offer Experiment

    Who was eligible and what counted

    Historically, 34% of accounts that took a promotion converted within 30 days, compared with 20% of nontakers. For a new promotion, the eligible population is active accounts without a recent promotion. Assign each enrolled account an offer or no offer and measure whether it converts within 30 days of assignment. Break-even requires a five-point increase.

    Activity in the previous month defines the randomization blocks. Opening happens after assignment. The primary analysis follows the assigned groups, including accounts that never opened an offer.

    What could explain the historical gap?

    The observed 14 points equal the effect of taking the promotion on historical takers (ATT) plus the difference between takers’ and nontakers’ average untreated potential outcomes. If takers would have converted at 29% without taking it, the decomposition would be 5 points of effect and 9 points of selection. The 29% is hypothetical.

    Why does an offer-to-all decision need the effect of assignment? The policy reaches every eligible enrolled account, including those who decline. Define Yi(1) and Yi(0) under offer and no offer. The enrolled intention-to-treat effect (ITT) averages Yi(1) − Yi(0) across that pool.

    The dashed arrow allows a direct offer-to-conversion path. Opening and conversion are generated separately in this trial, so the records alone leave mediation through opening unestablished. Use the Graph and adjustment exercise to trace the corresponding selection paths in historical records.

    Experiment design

    The MDE sets planned sensitivity against zero. Break-even defines the business threshold. To support clearing five points, the lower interval limit must exceed five. Assignment is 1:1 within baseline activity bands, with 30-day follow-up for every account.

    A comparison across baseline activity bands is a secondary, exploratory targeting question.

    Chance of clearing break-even

    This planning approximation uses independent binomial rates and a two-sided normal interval for the unadjusted difference. It uses the planned observed counts and holds the standard error at each assumed true lift. Realized Newcombe and block-adjusted intervals can differ.

    The worked defaults replay one fixed trial. Running with changed inputs generates a new simulated trial.

    Trial results

    Run the simulated trial to see the assignment ledger, conversion estimates, offer experiment results, and threats to the estimate.

    Assignment ledger

    Offer and control assignments were randomized separately within each baseline activity band. Reconcile the assignment log against the allocation ledger to verify that all assignments were recorded. Treatment assignment and analysis denominators are determined from the ledger.

    Baseline balance

    Continuous baseline characteristics can differ between assigned groups by chance, even when allocation is balanced within randomization blocks.

    Conversion estimates

    Analyze the accounts as assigned, including unopened offers. For the worked defaults the linear probability model regresses conversion on assignment and a higher-activity indicator. Its uncertainty uses HC2 standard errors, which adjust for unequal outcome variance and observation leverage, with a large-sample normal reference. Equal allocation fractions give the same point estimate as the crude difference.

    The pooled z-test and the Newcombe interval are separate unadjusted procedures. Compare the reported intervals to see how accounting for prognostic activity bands changes precision in this run. See A/B estimates and intervals for the individual rate intervals.

    Compare the effects in the two baseline activity bands

    The difference between band-specific effects has its own uncertainty. This exploratory comparison can motivate a separate targeting evaluation.

    Offer experiment results

    Compare the primary interval with zero and break-even. An interval spanning or touching a benchmark leaves that comparison unresolved. When follow-up is incomplete, also compare full-cohort bounds with both benchmarks.

    What assumptions would a complier effect require?

    A complier effect using assignment as an instrumental variable needs relevance, random assignment, exclusion of direct effects outside uptake, and monotonicity. The trial’s generator sets conversion by assignment and activity and generates openings separately. The assignment effect remains the policy contrast. Try Encouragement and uptake for a synthetic design with receipt driving outcomes.

    If stores adopted at different dates

    Compare each adoption cohort with stores still untreated over the same calendar period. An already-treated store can have a changing offer effect and make a poor comparison. The DiD cohort grid shows which estimates remain supported when you remove the never-treated stores.

    Related example: Search geo experiment

    The offer test estimates 30-day conversion lift for enrolled accounts. A Search spending decision needs evidence about a different intervention and outcome. The paired Search geo study compares assigned spend schedules and measures six-week incremental revenue per market.
