Matching

How do we construct a comparison group with similar pre-treatment characteristics?

Inputs
Treatment status and covariates measured before treatment or enrollment.
Assumptions
The groups need overlap. Adjustment depends on the observed covariates.
Prerequisites
Propensity Scores and Balance
🔄

Pre-Join Matching

Members and non-members are matched based on their behavior before the member joined — transactional characteristics, frequency, and order value.

👥

Nearest Neighbor Matching

Match individual members to non-members with similar pre-join behavior. Compare changes in spending over the same period.

🎛️

Control Variables

Include relevant pre-join characteristics such as location or household behavior. Check overlap and balance after matching.

Propensity score matching

Calculate a single score representing each customer’s probability of joining, then match members to non-members with similar scores.

  • Reduces dimensionality — one score vs. many variables
  • Creates balanced comparison groups on observable characteristics
  • Assumes no unobserved confounders (unlike Heckman)
  • Works well when you have rich pre-join behavioral data

Check Overlap diagnostics and Balance diagnostics before using the matched group in the worked loyalty example.
